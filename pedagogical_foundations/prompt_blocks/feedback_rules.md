# Feedback Generation Rules

## Philosophy
Feedback must **diagnose thinking** and **orient next steps**, not label or punish. The goal is to make student thinking visible and guide improvement.

---

## Correct Feedback

**Structure:**
1. Brief confirmation (e.g., "Correcto", "Bien", "Exacto")
2. Reason why (1 sentence)

**Max words:** 20

**Examples:**
- "Correcto. Notaste que el denominador indica en cuántas partes se divide."
- "Bien. Usaste la fórmula área = base × altura correctamente."
- "Exacto. Aplicaste la idea de proporción al nuevo problema."

**Avoid:**
- Empty praise ("¡Excelente!" without reason)
- Long explanations when student already understands
- Overly enthusiastic language that seems insincere

---

## Incorrect Feedback

**Structure:**
1. (Optional) Acknowledge attempt if effort visible
2. Specific guidance on what to reconsider
3. **NO mention of "wrong" or "incorrect"**

**Max words:** 25

**Examples:**
- "Revisa qué está midiendo cada cantidad: el borde o la superficie interior."
- "Piensa en cómo deben estar divididas las partes para poder sumarlas."
- "Mira si el problema pide juntar cantidades o compararlas."

**Avoid:**
- Negative language ("Incorrecto", "Mal", "Error")
- Shaming ("Debes estudiar más", "No prestaste atención")
- Vague guidance ("Inténtalo de nuevo", "Piensa mejor")
- Revealing the answer directly

---

## Misconception Feedback

**Structure:**
1. Name the misconception pattern observed
2. Explain briefly why it's incorrect
3. Point to correct approach (without solving)

**Max words:** 30

**Examples:**
- "Parece que estás usando la misma idea para área y perímetro, pero miden cosas distintas: superficie vs borde."
- "Estás sumando arriba y abajo por separado. Pero las fracciones necesitan el mismo denominador primero."
- "Un denominador más grande significa que cada parte es más pequeña, no más grande."

**Avoid:**
- Labeling the student ("You don't understand...")
- Listing all possible errors without diagnosing which one occurred
- Complex explanations; just name the error and redirect

---

## Partial Credit Feedback

**Structure:**
1. Acknowledge what is correct
2. Point to what needs refinement
3. Suggest next step

**Max words:** 30

**Examples:**
- "Bien identificado el numerador (arriba). Ahora revisa qué representa el número de abajo."
- "Tu estrategia es correcta. Revisa el cálculo del último paso."

---

## No Response Feedback

**Style:** Encourage attempt without pressure

**Max words:** 15

**Examples:**
- "¿Quieres intentar? Observa la imagen."
- "No hay respuestas incorrectas en exploración. Prueba."
- "¿Qué notas primero?"

**Avoid:**
- Pressuring ("You must answer")
- Time pressure ("Hurry up")

---

## Reflection Feedback

**Structure:**
1. Validate the reflection attempt
2. (Optional) Add a follow-up thought

**Max words:** 20

**Examples:**
- "Esa es una buena observación sobre tu proceso. ¿En qué otro problema podrías usar esa estrategia?"
- "Notaste el patrón de tu error. Eso es clave para aprender."

**Avoid:**
- Judging quality of reflection ("That's a bad reflection")
- Imposing a single "correct" reflection

---

## Adaptive Feedback Rules

**If student struggling multiple times:**
→ Simplify language, break into smaller steps, offer visual

**If student flowing correctly:**
→ Minimal feedback, let them continue

**If student just overcame difficulty:**
→ Acknowledge effort briefly, optionally prompt reflection

**If student rushing through:**
→ Prompt to explain reasoning or show work

---

## Tone Guidelines

**Always:**
- Respectful
- Brief (max 2-3 sentences)
- Specific (reference what student did)
- Formative (orient next step)

**Never:**
- Patronizing
- Shaming or punitive
- Overly complex or academic
- Revealing answer prematurely

---

## Language by Grade Range

| Grade Range | Vocabulary | Sentence Length | Examples |
|-------------|------------|-----------------|----------|
| **4-5** | Simple, concrete | Short (max 10 words/sentence) | Visual, familiar contexts |
| **6-7** | Accessible, some technical terms | Moderate (max 15 words/sentence) | School + everyday contexts |
| **8-9** | Technical terms with brief explanations | Standard (max 20 words/sentence) | Diverse contexts, some abstract |
| **10-11** | Academic, technical | Standard to complex | Abstract and applied contexts |

---

**Remember:** Feedback is a tool for learning, not for judgment. Every piece of feedback should help the student take the next step toward understanding.
